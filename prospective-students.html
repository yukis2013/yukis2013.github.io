Shigemoto Lab · Counseling Psychology

Thinking about applying? Start here.

This page describes what our lab works on and what mentorship here looks like, so you can judge for yourself whether this is the right fit before you apply.

Admissions status

Accepting doctoral students for Fall 2027. Deadlines and requirements are posted on the UF Counseling Psychology program site.

Fit

Who tends to thrive in this lab

Students who fit well in this lab tend to share the following approaches and attitudes toward research.

Interest in our research

Our lab studies individual and community resilience after adversity, including disaster, trauma, and public health crises. Naturally, we are looking for students who go beyond a broad interest in "trauma" or "resilience": students who can point to a specific paper or project of ours and think about what questions or hypotheses it raises for them.

Self-direction

You take initiative, manage your own timelines, and when you hit something you do not understand, your first instinct is to look it up, try something, and then bring me a specific question. I am here to advise and to work through hard problems with you. I am not here to assign you your next step each week.

Willingness to learn quantitative methods

Much of our work uses longitudinal, multilevel, and structural equation modeling. You do not need to be strong in statistics when you arrive, but you do need to be genuinely willing to learn.

Serious commitment to research during training

Counseling psychology trains both scientists and practitioners, and students here go on to a range of careers. Students who fit this lab are those who invest seriously in research throughout their training, rather than treating it as a requirement to clear. Students considering research-oriented careers are especially welcome.

Mentorship

How we work together

What you can expect from me

  • Regular meetings
  • Hands-on training in quantitative methods, including designs you will not get from coursework alone
  • Support in moving projects toward conference presentations and publication
  • Direct, specific feedback on your work
  • Career guidance. If you are heading toward a path I know less well, I will do my best to connect you with people who know it better

What I expect from you

  • Come to meetings prepared, with specific questions and your progress
  • Own your projects, including the parts that are tedious
  • Tell me when something is stuck or slipping, early rather than late
  • Take feedback objectively, as being about the work (easier said than done)
  • Support the other students in the lab

Applying

Admission decisions are made by the UF Counseling Psychology program as a whole, on the basis of your application materials. Emailing me first will not improve your chances, and applying without contacting me will not hurt them. So there is no need to contact me before you apply. Deadlines, required materials, and eligibility are all described on the program page.

The best place to tell me that you are interested in this lab and want to do research together is your application itself. That said, if you have read this page and still have a question, you are welcome to write to me at yshigemoto@ufl.edu, in English or in Japanese. If our work interests you, please apply.
