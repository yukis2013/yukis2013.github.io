Shigemoto Lab

Research

Our lab studies how individuals and communities recover and grow after adversity, such as disasters, trauma, and public health crises. Our work is organized around three questions, described below.

How people grow after adversity

We study how people adapt and grow after trauma and adversity. Our work focuses on personal growth initiative, rumination, and coping, and how these relate to posttraumatic growth over time. Some of our studies follow people day by day using smartphones.

Selected papers

  • Shigemoto, Y., & Bhattarai, M. (2025). Longitudinal association between event-related rumination and posttraumatic growth: Latent growth curve mediation model. Stress and Health, 41(4), e70067. https://doi.org/10.1002/smi.70067
  • Shigemoto, Y., White, A.*, & Baloch, R.* (2025). Effect of engagement in personal growth initiative skills on depression, anxiety, and stress: Multilevel mediation analysis with intensive longitudinal data. Journal of Clinical Psychology, 81, 7–18. https://doi.org/10.1002/jclp.23749
  • Shigemoto, Y. (2022). Association between daily rumination and posttraumatic growth during the COVID-19 pandemic: An experience sampling method. Psychological Trauma: Theory, Research, Practice, and Policy, 14(2), 229–236. https://doi.org/10.1037/tra0001061

Who has resources, and who loses them

After a disaster or crisis, people lose resources such as housing, income, and social support. We study who is more likely to lose these resources and how unequal access to resources affects recovery.

Selected papers

  • Shigemoto, Y., & Giniewski, W.* (2025). Exploring patterns of short- and mid-term essential resource loss following Hurricane Ike: A mixture modeling approach. Progress in Disaster Science, 28, 100481. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.pdisas.2025.100481

How communities recover and grow together

We study how communities shape recovery after hurricanes and public health crises. Our work looks at community resilience, social cohesion, and where people live. We are especially interested in how communities recover and grow together after disasters.

Current projects

Coping during the COVID-19 pandemic

Using data collected over three years of the COVID-19 pandemic, we are comparing different ways of understanding coping flexibility and how each relates to mental health over time.

College students' resources and wellbeing

A person-centered study of how college students use different resources and how this relates to their wellbeing. Graduate and undergraduate members of the lab are leading this work.

2025 California wildfires

A survey study of people affected by the 2025 California wildfires. Graduate and undergraduate members of the lab are examining resource loss, community resilience, and growth.

Measuring distress among older Asian Americans

A study of how well the HSCL-25, a widely used measure of anxiety and depression symptoms, works for older Asian Americans, with implications for community screening. Graduate members of the lab are part of this work.

Wellbeing across four countries

Using national survey data from Japan, the United States, Germany, and Sweden, we are examining how factors related to wellbeing differ across countries.
